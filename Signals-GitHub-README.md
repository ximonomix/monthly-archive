# Signals

**Augmentatism Discord · Community atlas · 12 November 2026**  
**Comparable historical baseline:** October 2025–September 2026  
**Partial supplementary snapshot:** 1 October–12 November 2026

> **Preserve the many voices, not one verdict.** This atlas describes where conversations happened, who participated, what subjects appeared, and how people responded. It is not a ranking of expertise, an assessment of factual accuracy, or a substitute for the original Discord discussions.

**At a glance:** **21,014** recorded messages · **17,163** human-authored · **3,851** automated · **1,394** distinct human accounts in four comparable quarterly exports.

The charts below are the original high-resolution graphics from the [*Signals* PDF](#about-this-edition), paired with GitHub-native text and tables. All image paths are relative, so they render directly on GitHub when this folder is uploaded intact.

### Channel trajectories

![Heatmap comparing channel activity in the four quarterly exports](assets/channel-trajectories.png)

For the channel charts, messages written inside public threads are grouped under the **parent channel**. That is why these totals can differ from lists that count parent-channel messages and individual threads separately.

In Q3, for example, `#local-hardware-lab` recorded **2,179** human posts including its public threads, while `#ai-models` recorded **1,951**. These are signals of where discussions gathered—not assessments of the value of the contributions.

## 4. Participation and continuity

![Number of contributors present in one, two, three or four quarters](assets/participation-continuity.png)

Across all four complete quarters:

- **1,394** distinct non-bot accounts posted in the collected sources.
- **1,290** appeared in only one quarter; **87** in two; **12** in three; and **5** in all four.
- **104** appeared in at least two quarters, and they authored **9,263 messages**, approximately **54.0%** of human-authored posts.

The result describes a recurring core alongside many occasional voices. Posting in one quarter only does not establish that someone left the community, and a high posting count is not a measure of authority or contribution quality.

## 5. Subjects and named-term signals

![Comparative heatmap of selected named term mentions across the quarters](assets/named-term-signals.png)

These counts measure the number of eligible **human discussion messages containing a term**. A message with several mentions of the same term counts once for that term; a message containing two different tracked terms counts once for each. Onboarding channels and automated content are excluded from this analysis.

For example, the Qwen term appears in **2** eligible messages in Q1 2026, **46** in Q2, and **548** in Q3. The RTX 5090 term appears in **0**, **18**, and **176**, respectively. This describes attention, not technical merit or endorsement.

### Provisional theme indicators

![Overlapping keyword-based topic indicators from 2025 Q4 to 2026 Q3](assets/theme-indicators.png)

Themes such as models, local hardware, automation, memory, creative media and governance are **keyword indicators**. They can overlap, miss paraphrases or include false positives. ## 10. How to read Signals

This analysis intentionally remains light on editorial judgment:

- **Participation:** counts what people posted, without ranking their knowledge or worth.
- **Topics:** counts terms and descriptive keyword patterns, without deciding which opinions are valid.
- **Reactions:** records visible community response, without equating it to truth or consensus.
- **Traceability:** retains a route back to the originating Discord discussions wherever links are available.
- **Respect for absences:** private channels, deleted messages, audio/video content, and silent members are beyond the evidence available here.

The intended use is to help readers find conversations, understand their context, and form their own assessments—not to produce a single authoritative interpretation of the community.
